A Family Story

Dad's Quarter

Satchel Paige, Eddie Gaedel, Bud Thomas — and one lucky, tarnished quarter that ties it all together.

Life Goes On

Back in the early forties, baseball was not yet integrated. In fact, many of the best players were in the Negro Leagues, and Satchel Paige was one of them. Some say he was the best pitcher ever to play the game.

Baseball was a little more flamboyant in those days, and Satchel was known to tell his infielders to just sit down — which they did — and then strike out the side anyway. He was already famous, and as times changed, he eventually made it to the Major Leagues, pitching for the St. Louis Browns.

… Dad nearly lost his quarter…

Dad's Quarter and Satchel Paige

One lucky day, when Dad was just a boy, he traveled to St. Louis to see a game. Satchel Paige was there. Like all young boys, he wanted a better look, so he went down by the dugouts. When he was there, Dad dropped a quarter. It landed in the dugout, and Satchel Paige picked it up and put it in his pocket. Dad said nothing — but he sadly thought the great Satchel Paige had just stolen his quarter.

After a minute or two, Satchel Paige turned around and, with a teasing grin, handed Dad back his quarter. Dad cherished that memory, and liked to tell me this story.

Dad was a fan of both Satchel Paige and the St. Louis Browns.

… Dad also told me the story of Eddie Gaedel…

Eddie Gaedel

To gain fans and keep the game entertaining, the St. Louis Browns once hired a dwarf. His name was Eddie Gaedel. He weighed 65 pounds and stood 3 feet 7 inches tall. He was placed on the roster on August 19, 1951, and sent up to bat.

The strike zone runs roughly from the shoulders to the knees — and on a batter that small, his strike zone measured only about an inch and a half. He batted just once in his career, and in four pitches, he was walked. Baseball fans still love this story. So did his teammates.

… but even Dad didn't know about his teammate that day…

John Thomas

I was well past school age when I learned that Eddie Gaedel's teammate that day was a shortstop named John Thomas. John Thomas played alongside both Satchel Paige and Eddie Gaedel. He was also once coached by Babe Ruth and Ty Cobb. He played only a single year in the majors, but batted an outstanding .350. I knew John “Bud” Thomas.

Bud Thomas later went on to study education and became a grade school principal. Mr. Thomas was my grade school principal. I liked him.

I hope that all who remember him will look up his obituary and hear his own words in the documentary linked at the bottom of this page.

… and as it happened…

The Catch

Mr. Thomas loved baseball. And yet his obituary notes that he rarely talked about it, because he was more proud of his work as a teacher, principal, and school administrator.

In the mornings before school, he would take a softball and bat and hit pop-ups to his “kids.” I was there, and I know that every child on that playground relished the time. His years as a major league player, and everything else he accomplished, shouldn't be forgotten — but this is my memory of him.

Some of his hits went very far, and every kid dreamed of catching one hit well into the outfield. I was a young boy, and I always wanted to catch one.

… and eventually…

The Exaggeration

I caught the ball! “Did you see that?!”

My fame didn't last long. The school bell rang, and I walked off the playground toward the school door. As I opened it, there on the ground was an old, tarnished quarter.

Dad's quarter! The one from Satchel Paige! When I picked it up, my mind was suddenly filled with images — things I had never seen. Satchel Paige. Eddie Gaedel.

…okay, I made that last part up.

Everything else is true. Satchel Paige, Eddie Gaedel, Bud Thomas, and Dad are all gone now — but in some strange, connected way, they created the story I remember today. Now we are all creating the stories. Live them well.
